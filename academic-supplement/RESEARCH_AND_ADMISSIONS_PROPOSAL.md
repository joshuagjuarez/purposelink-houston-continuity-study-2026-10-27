I bring a functioning six-host, seven-placement testbed; a versioned record of results, failures, and corrections; the technical capacity to maintain it; and a clear research question about accountable continuity across sessions. ## What exists now

The frozen public record documents six physical hosts and seven compute placements. Its paired test crossed six synthetic cases, two continuity conditions, and two sessions: 24 planned cells. Nineteen returned, five were held, and only two met the literal strict scorer—PL-S04 session 1 in both arms. Session 2 had zero strict passes in either arm, so the record establishes no continuity benefit. Some responses looked useful while failing exact contracts, which makes the testbed valuable for studying the difference between plausible language and accountable performance.

Separate from those model results, 32 curated native checks passed with zero model calls. A synthetic casebook contains 30 episodes across six fictional caseworker contexts and five time points. Those episodes were not real families, students, services, or clinical outcomes. No agency was contacted. Hardware, model, output limits, interventions, scheduling, and capacity were not controlled well enough for causal claims. These limitations are not a footnote; they define the next research design.

The post-study documentary layer adds 11 screenshot observations and a raw memo transcript, retained privately. A recording-list screenshot displays nine starts and durations totaling 46 minutes 37 seconds. The clip-to-text matches remain provisional because no original audio or clip export identifiers were supplied. This discipline—preserving what exists while marking what cannot be certified—is part of the contribution I want to strengthen in an academic setting.

Screenshots also contain references to Joshua’s Tree, the Institute for Paternal Reform, and an earlier 12-month initiative. They are preserved as screenshot observations and editorial context, not evidence of corporations, accreditation, validated programs, or PurposeLink study results.

## Questions suitable for supervised study

1. Under a preregistered rubric, does a bounded continuity mechanism improve recall of current facts and corrections across sessions without preserving superseded or unnecessary personal information?
2. Can minimum-necessary handoffs improve resource verification and escalation—checking geography, eligibility, availability, ownership, and uncertainty—without presenting a directory pointer as a completed referral?
3. How reliably can an AI-supported simulation recognize role-play ambiguity, preserve injected variables as declared variables, and pause for human review rather than turning scenario language into claims about real people?

Learning benefit remains unmeasured. A later education study could ask whether inspecting failures improves student reasoning, but only with faculty design, appropriate approval, and a valid comparison—not by relabeling these engineering artifacts as student results.

## A bounded proposed phase

I propose a six-to-eight-week, synthetic-first pilot, contingent on a faculty liaison and institutional approval. In weeks 1–2, we would scope the question, define roles and data boundaries, select only synthetic cases, preregister a rubric, and specify stop/go conditions. No private biography or real client data would enter by default.

In weeks 3–5, we would freeze cases and settings, run balanced repetitions, log corrections and minimum-necessary handoffs, and have an independent reviewer apply the rubric. Resource claims would be verified as claims about public information, not treated as service delivery. Human supervisors would retain decisions and escalation authority.

In weeks 6–8, students could review de-identified synthetic outputs only if faculty and the institution approve that participation. The team would publish a reproducible evaluation, an error taxonomy, and a go/no-go report. A no-go finding would be a legitimate result. Advancement toward any real-world study would require a separate protocol, consent and privacy review, professional supervision, and explicit safeguards.

I would lead technical implementation, maintain the existing testbed, document provenance, and co-design with faculty and social-work practitioners. I would also train participating students on the bounded tooling under faculty oversight. I am willing to share relevant materials under agreed research and privacy terms. I am not offering an unconditional personal-data gift, promising unpaid support forever, or proposing automated casework.

During an agreed funded term, the project could make participation available without a participant subscription. Engineering, training, support, security, and evaluation should be budgeted separately; any ongoing institutional license would be a separate agreement. That separation protects research independence and avoids disguising labor or infrastructure costs.
